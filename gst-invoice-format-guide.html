GST invoice format in India: what a valid tax invoice needs

A tax invoice is the document that lets your client claim input tax credit and lets you report the supply. If a particular is missing, the invoice can be treated as defective and the credit can be denied — so the fields matter more than the design.

Updated 26 October 2026 · 7 minute read

The particulars below come from Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, which sets out what a tax invoice must contain. Thresholds and procedural rules change from time to time, so treat this as a working summary and confirm the current position with your accountant.

The particulars Rule 46 asks for

ParticularWhat it means in practice
Supplier's name, address and GSTINYour registered legal name, principal place of business, and 15-character GSTIN. A trade name alone is not enough.
Consecutive serial numberUp to 16 characters, alphanumeric plus / and -, unique within a financial year, in one or more series.
Date of issueThe date the invoice is issued, not the date the work started.
Recipient's name, address and GSTINRequired when the recipient is registered. For an unregistered recipient, name and address; the delivery address too if the invoice value is ₹50,000 or more.
HSN code or SACGoods use HSN, services use SAC. Digits required depend on turnover — 4, 6 or 8.
Description, quantity, valueWhat was supplied, how much, and the total value per line.
Taxable value and discountValue after any discount, and the discount shown separately if it is not already reflected in the price.
Rate and amount of taxThe GST rate and the tax amount, split as CGST + SGST or shown as IGST.
Place of supplyRequired for inter-state supplies and where the recipient is outside India. It decides whether you charge IGST or CGST + SGST.
Address of deliveryWhere it differs from the recipient's address.
Signature or digital signatureOf the supplier or an authorised representative.
QR codeRequired in the cases the rules specify, such as B2C invoices above the notified turnover threshold.

CGST + SGST or IGST?

The deciding factor is the place of supply relative to your own state.

  • Same state — an intra-state supply. The GST rate is split evenly between CGST (central) and SGST (state). An 18% supply shows 9% CGST and 9% SGST.
  • Different state — an inter-state supply. The full rate is charged as IGST. An 18% supply shows 18% IGST.
  • Union territory — CGST plus UTGST instead of SGST.
  • Export or supply to a SEZ unit — normally zero-rated, with the invoice carrying the prescribed endorsement.

Getting this wrong is the single most common GST invoicing error: an 18% invoice split into CGST and SGST for a client in another state is a defective invoice, and the client cannot take the credit without asking you to revise it.

Invoice numbering rules

Keep it boring and consistent:

  • One series per financial year, or several clearly identifiable series — for example one per GSTIN if you have multiple registrations.
  • No gaps within a series. If you cancel an invoice, keep the number and mark the invoice cancelled rather than deleting it.
  • Maximum 16 characters. Letters, digits, hyphens and slashes are allowed.
  • Unique — never reuse a number that has already been issued.

INV-2026-001 is fine. So is ADV/26-27/001. What matters is that the series is unbroken and traceable.

Tax invoice, bill of supply, or something else

DocumentWhen you issue it
Tax invoiceTaxable supply of goods or services to a registered or unregistered buyer.
Bill of supplyExempt or nil-rated supplies, and composition-scheme dealers. No tax is charged, so it is not a tax invoice.
Credit noteSales return, rate revision or a post-invoice discount that reduces the value.
Debit noteValue or tax on an earlier invoice went up.
Proforma invoiceAn advance request or quotation confirmation. Not a tax invoice and not valid for credit.

E-invoicing and the QR code

Businesses above the notified aggregate turnover must generate an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) from the Invoice Registration Portal for B2B invoices, and the QR code becomes part of the document. The turnover threshold has been lowered in stages over the years, so check the figure that applies to you for the current year rather than relying on an older number.

If you are below the threshold, a normal tax invoice with the particulars above is what you need — and if you ever cross it, your invoicing software has to support IRN generation, which this simple generator does not do.

Mistakes that cost you the credit

  • Client's GSTIN missing or mistyped. A B2B invoice without a valid GSTIN usually means the client cannot claim credit, and you cannot report it as a B2B supply.
  • Wrong tax split. CGST/SGST on an inter-state supply, or the reverse.
  • Tax charged on the pre-discount value. GST is on the taxable value after discount.
  • No HSN or SAC. Required, and increasingly validated at filing.
  • Place of supply blank on an inter-state invoice.
  • Numbering gaps. Deleting a draft invoice instead of cancelling it breaks the series.
  • Round-figure tax. Let the software compute the tax; do not hand-round the line.
A note on scope. This is a summary for general guidance, not tax advice. Rates, thresholds and e-invoicing rules change — confirm the current requirements for your turnover, state and category of supply with a qualified professional before you rely on them.
